# Exercise demonstrations and sources

All demonstration files are stored in this website's `media/` directory. Offline playback does not require a connection to the original source website.

The source videos have been checked for exercise identity, equipment variations, licence information and selected frames. This is not a frame-by-frame assessment by a qualified trainer, and it does not mean every demonstration suits every person. Filmed demonstrations, source animations and original movement diagrams are labelled separately.

Video edits consist of trimming, resizing, removing audio, converting formats and extracting posters. No AI-generated frames have been added to the filmed demonstrations. GIFs converted from source clips sample actual frames, typically at 10 fps, up to 320 pixels wide and about seven seconds long. MP4 versions retain clearer continuous movement.

Modified CC BY-SA assets retain their original CC BY-SA licence; CC BY assets retain the listed licence. Other assets are used under their stated terms. No creator or organisation endorses this website. U.S. Department of Defense and Marine Corps material does not imply official endorsement.

## Instruction and programme references

- [ACE — Resistance Training: Beginner Home and Gym-based Workouts](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/blog/8650/resistance-training-workouts-for-beginners/) — Beginner whole-body training 2–3 nonconsecutive days per week.
- [ACSM — 2026 Resistance Training Guidelines](https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/) — Consistency, individual progression; advanced techniques and failure are optional.
- [ACE Exercise Library](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/) — Cross-checks of setup, controlled range and movement descriptions. Exact exercise references are linked in data.mjs where available.
- [wger source library and licensing](https://github.com/wger-project/wger) — Individual media records retain author and licence metadata; downloaded on 2026-09-18.

The programmes are mainly adapted from the user-provided Cantonese notes and are not presented as the original creator's official programmes. The beginner full-body schedule now uses nonconsecutive training days. Overgeneralisations have been removed, including a mandatory switch to a split after a fixed number of weeks, machines always being safer, and knees never travelling past toes.
